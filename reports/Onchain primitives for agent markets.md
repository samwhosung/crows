# Ship the resolver primitive, borrow everything else

Building a protocol primitive rather than a platform is the right call in October 2026, and the evidence is lopsided. Nearly every layer a code-resolved conditional contract needs is already on mainnet and cheap. A zkVM proof of a billion-cycle program costs about four cents on Boundless and verifies on-chain for roughly 270k gas. Signed price reports are free to pull. Resource-lock escrows with pluggable arbiters (Uniswap's The Compact) and evaluator escrows (ERC-8183) are deployed. Agents already have rails for identity (ERC-8004), payments (x402) and bounded permissions (ERC-7715/7710). Meanwhile, agent platforms that were held up by incentives have collapsed: Virtuals' ACP went from 814k jobs a month to about 6k, and its September fees were $5.48. A follow-up novelty check on Oct 21 covered DefiLlama's 135 prediction-market listings, about 125 GitHub queries, and contract source for every candidate. It found no live, used product that combines the four pieces the user wants: permissionless market creation, an arbitrary resolver program supplied by the creator, two-sided stakes, and resolution that anyone can trigger. The *mechanism* is not new. Dormant 2020–23 frameworks (Opium, Ante, Cozy) had all four properties. Gnosis CTF and Hedgehog allowed oracles chosen by the creator. Sapience's audited 2026 escrow accepts any resolver contract, yet its first deployment shows zero third-party resolvers. Each attempt ended gated, trusted in practice, or unused. The novelty is therefore not the hook. It is making creator-written resolvers discoverable, trustworthy and liquid for agents. The evidence supports a thin settlement kernel: escrow-first atomic matching; a resolver committed by hash that runs natively or is proven in a RISC-V zkVM; inputs accepted only through typed adapters that resist manipulation; and INVALID-plus-refund in place of token votes. Around the kernel sit facades (ERC-8183 events, ERC-7683 `resolve()`, x402 discovery, an MCP server) that let existing agent tooling find it without new integration work. The strongest starting market is **existence claims** ("some input exists such that P holds"). They are cheap to prove, naturally two-sided, and immune to AI-generated junk submissions, and they fill a gap that human-triaged bug bounties and test-suite procurement are visibly failing to cover. The hard failure modes are real, but most of them give way to architecture rather than curation: resolver bugs, a description that doesn't match the resolver, manipulation of inputs at the deadline, latency races, Sybil reputation and harm markets.

## Protocol-first wins because only one layer is missing

The record from 2025 and 2026 splits cleanly. On one side are agent *platforms* whose volume came from incentives; on the other are minimal *primitives* with pluggable parts. Virtuals' ACP v1 processed 3.7M jobs, then fell from **814k jobs in March 2026 to roughly 5–7k a month from June to September** once its Revenue Network payouts stopped. September platform fees were **$5.48**. Its ERC-8183-based v2 settled about **$2,660 in six months**, and 64% of its Base volume came from two wallets paying each other ([ChainWard](https://chainward.ai/decodes/virtuals-top-dog)). x402 claims more than 100M payments. A Visa × Artemis adjustment strips out **about 89% of raw volume as wash or test traffic**, leaving about $15M lifetime, and the July 2026 run-rate was roughly $20k a day ([CURB](https://curb.numetal.xyz/dispatch/x402-economy-july-2026)). ERC-8004 counts **841,978 registered agents and was adding 110k a day** on Oct 21 ([8004scan](https://8004scan.io/)). Yet only 3–15% expose a live service, 59–91% of reviewers look like Sybils, and pushing an agent's score past the 90 threshold costs a median **$0.0027 on Base** ([arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26028v2)). The crypto agent frameworks also churned: GOAT SDK is archived ([GitHub](https://github.com/goat-sdk/goat)), and major DeFi protocols now ship MCP servers and "skills" that build unsigned transactions for general-purpose agents instead ([Morpho](https://morpho.org/blog/introducing-morpho-agents-beta-interface-built-for-ai-agents); [Base](https://base.org/blog/the-agentic-economy-is-here)).

The primitives that compounded share one shape: a small immutable core with a pluggable judgment slot. Morpho Blue's permissionless isolated markets grew past **$17B in deposits** and now serve as the lending backbone distributed by Coinbase, Robinhood and Circle ([Morpho](https://morpho.org/blog/the-morpho-effect-september-2026)). The Compact is an ownerless ERC-6909 resource-lock contract in which signed commitments name an **arbiter** that verifies conditions. It sits at the same address on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and Unichain ([Uniswap](https://blog.uniswap.org/the-compact-v1)). The Gnosis Conditional Token Framework (CTF) treats the oracle as **just an address that calls `reportPayouts` with a payout vector**, and it supports nested positions across conditions ([CTF guide](https://github.com/gnosis/conditional-tokens-contracts/blob/master/docs/developer-guide.rst)). For agents, what makes a protocol usable is a stable contract surface plus machine-readable specs, not a front-end.

The layer still missing is exactly the user's: a **two-sided, permissionlessly resolvable conditional stake**. ERC-8183 is the closest standard. It is one-sided (a client pays a provider), only its evaluator can complete a job, and its own spec says **"no dispute resolution exists… reject and expiry are final"** ([EIP-8183](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8183)). In the Ethereum Magicians thread, contributors argue that the standard should be an `IArbiter`-style condition interface, and Sentinel proposes a permissionless `executeEvaluation` to fix evaluator liveness ([Ethereum Magicians](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-8183-agentic-commerce/27902?page=2)). Both are moves toward the user's design. Arkhai's Alkahest has the right semantics: obligation → arbiter → escrow, with composable `AllArbiter`/`AnyArbiter` combinators and outcomes attested via EAS. But it has 11 GitHub stars and lists no deployed addresses ([GitHub](https://github.com/arkhai-io/alkahest)). A `job-escrow` scheme proposed for x402 on Oct 5 has had no maintainer response ([x402 #3694](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/issues/3694)). The redesigned ERC-7683 explicitly drops the per-order escrow assumption and standardizes a view-only `resolve()` instead ([EIP-7683](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7683)). The standards community has drawn the outline of the gap; nobody has filled it.

The best case for a platform deserves a fair hearing. MetaDAO's sales were **13.8× oversubscribed**, yet its permissionless track saw only 9 of 90 launches clear their minimum. Curation, not the mechanism, decided quality ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/metadao-raises-625m-in-23-sales-faces-challenge-of-filtering-quality-projects)). (The source's summary says $454M committed while its body says $624.7M; the body figure is used here.) Polymarket's Protocol V2 is heading toward pluggable resolution with an **OracleAggregator** and "one exchange for many market types." It is permissioned and governance-upgradeable, rolling out with a tentative Nov 2 cutover ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-smart-contract-overhaul-protocol-v2)). Liquidity also fragments: semantically equivalent markets across venues show **2–4% persistent price gaps** because they share no event identity ([arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706)). These are real arguments for someone running discovery, risk scoring and curation. They are not arguments for putting those functions into settlement. A protocol can make event identity canonical by construction, defining the event ID as the hash of resolver image, parameters, input sources and deadline. Front-ends and curators can then compete above a shared liquidity identity, which is how Morpho's 30+ curators sit above Blue.

Composition needs dependency hygiene, because 2026 was hard on the things people compose with. Lattice shut down Redstone on May 15, 2026 ([Lattice](https://x.com/latticexyz/status/2044103611072835744)). Boundless's Kailua ZK-fraud-proof repo is archived with no further security fixes ([GitHub](https://github.com/boundless-xyz/kailua)). Truebit was killed by a $26M overflow in an auxiliary 2021 token contract, not its verification game ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384861/truebit-26-million-exploit)). Arbitrum paused new Stylus activations on Oct 2 ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/security-council-emergency-action-2-10-2026/31530)). ORA and Opacity pivoted away from the products people built on ([ora.io](https://www.ora.io/); [opacitylabs.com](https://opacitylabs.com/)). Chainlink Functions shut down on mainnet on June 30, 2026 ([Chainlink migration guide](https://docs.chain.link/cre/reference/clf-migration-ts)); one search summary said Sept 1, but the directly fetched doc says June 30. The ERC-8004 proxies on Ethereum and Base are **owned by a single EOA with no multisig or timelock**, and the Reputation proxy has already been upgraded twice ([LLM4Agents](https://llm4agents.com/blog/erc-8004-registries-onchain-audit)). The rule follows: the settlement kernel depends only on verifier contracts and signature schemes. Every other integration is either an optional surface or an input dependency declared in market metadata, and the fallback is INVALID-and-refund.

Demand points the same way. Organic agent-to-agent *service* commerce on ACP turned out to be roughly $100–$500 a month once incentives stopped ([ChainWard](https://chainward.ai/decodes/virtuals-top-dog)). Agents as *risk-takers* are older and more organic: Olas runs 448 daily active agents ([olas.network](https://olas.network/)), and its Polystrat agent made more than 4,200 Polymarket trades in its first month ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/03/15/ai-agents-are-quietly-rewriting-prediction-market-trading)). The real "outside money" comes from parties who pay for information or security. Numerai's roughly $700M fund pays its stake-and-burn tournament ([Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/gb/learn/what-is-numeraire-nmr)). Immunefi paid **$13.45M for 837 valid bugs in H1 2026** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/407707/crypto-hack-losses-fall-below-1-billion-in-h1-2026-even-as-attack-volume-hits-record-immunefi)). Optimism and the Uniswap Foundation subsidized conditional funding markets ([Uniswap Foundation](https://www.uniswapfoundation.org/blog/conditional-funding-markets-launch-for-unichain-lending-protocols)). The protocol should be designed to attract those sponsors and should never pay for volume.

### Verifiable compute turned resolution into a cents-per-run problem

General-purpose RISC-V zkVMs are production infrastructure. The cost of proving an Ethereum block fell from **$1.69 to under one cent** during 2025, and single-GPU proving fell from 16 minutes to under 60 seconds ([Ethproofs review](https://hackmd.io/@willcorcoran/S1A840ZMZg)). SP1 Hypercube went to mainnet in February 2026 and is used by Optimism, Base and Chainlink CCIP 2.0 ([Succinct blog](https://blog.succinct.xyz/)). OpenVM 2.1 proves mainnet blocks on four RTX 5090s in **5.0 s on average and 9.7 s at P99**, and its RV32IM and Keccak/SHA-2 circuits are formally verified in Lean ([Axiom](https://axiom.xyz/blog/2-1)). An SP1 Groth16 proof costs **~270k gas** to verify, and PLONK costs ~300k ([SP1 docs](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/generating-proofs/proof-types)). At Boundless's project-reported median of **$0.04 per billion cycles** ([Boundless](https://boundlessfdn.org/blog/)), a 50M-cycle test-suite run costs about a fifth of a cent and a 100B-cycle run about $4, before premiums. Proving cost is noise compared with any plausible stake. Latency is the real constraint: deadlines should budget minutes to an hour, not block times.

Boundless is already a primitive conditional contract. A request names a guest image, an input, a **predicate on the output journal**, an optional **callback to the requester's contract**, and an offer that rises from a minimum to a maximum price. The prover who locks the request posts collateral, which is slashed if the deadline is missed ([Boundless docs](https://docs.boundless.network/developers/proof-lifecycle)). A resolver contract can therefore post "prove image R on committed input D" and receive settlement by callback. One structural limit matters: in Boundless, Succinct ([Succinct docs](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/provers/how-it-works/overview)) and Brevis, **the requester supplies the input**. That fits deterministic resolvers, but it does not fit markets where the taker's private witness is the point, because delegating the proof leaks the witness to the prover. Those markets need local proving.

For heavy resolvers, the 2025–26 consensus is a hybrid: optimistic by default, with disputes settled by a single ZK proof. Under Kailua's design the worst case was about 100B cycles, roughly $100 and about an hour, with the loser paying ([Kailua](https://github.com/boundless-xyz/kailua)). Arbitrum BoLD is the production-grade permissionless bisection game, but it needs a **3,600 ETH assertion bond** and a 7-day window that can stretch to 14 ([Arbitrum docs](https://docs.arbitrum.io/how-arbitrum-works/bold/bold-economics-of-disputes)). Cartesi PRT is the only shipped option for full Linux workloads, and it reached L2BEAT Stage 2 despite an October 2025 liveness freeze ([Cartesi](https://docs.cartesi.io/fraud-proofs/prt/prt-introduction/)). Pure bisection remains the right tool only for code a zkVM cannot run.

The soundness surface is the sobering part. Production zkVMs claim about **100-bit** provable security, with 128 bits still a target. Soundness bugs keep turning up: in RISC Zero, in Jolt in May 2026, and in stwo-cairo in June 2026, the last found by Axiom's **multi-agent AI auditor** ([Axiom](https://axiom.xyz/blog/stwo-bug)). The most instructive incident came in April 2026. Trail of Bits produced a valid proof of a false claim under Google's SP1 verification key. It did not break SP1; it exploited **bugs in the guest program**, namely unchecked deserialization and register aliasing, which it found by reverse-engineering the RISC-V ELF in Ghidra ([Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/04/17/we-beat-googles-zero-knowledge-proof-of-quantum-cryptanalysis/)). "A valid proof only shows the program ran." In this protocol, **the resolver is the attack surface**, and the user's reverse-engineering background is directly relevant to the most important audit job. SP1 also warns that `cargo prove build` is not reproducible by default ([SP1 docs](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/writing-programs/compiling)), so Docker-pinned builds that hash to the image ID must be mandatory.

Verifiable AI splits into what can be proven and what can only be re-executed. DeepProve proves GPT-2 at about 174 tokens/min and Gemma 3 at about 86 ([ePrint 2026/1112](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1112)), so a frontier-class judge cannot be zk-proven in 2026. EigenAI gets **bit-exact LLM inference at 1.8% overhead** on fixed GPU SKUs. The fixed SKU is a hard requirement: A100 and H100 outputs matched 0% of the time. The EigenAI paper names prediction-market adjudication as a target use ([EigenAI paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00182v1)). Gensyn's REE achieves cross-hardware reproducibility ([Gensyn](https://www.gensyn.ai/news/ree)). Its **Delphi** markets, live since May 7, 2026, are "settled by AI", with per-market non-upgradeable contracts and an Agent SDK, and they have **no formal dispute process** ([Gensyn](https://www.gensyn.ai/news/building-delphi-pricing-settlement-and-agentic-trading)). Delphi is the closest prior art for AI-judged markets. The credible LLM-judge recipe pins weights, prompt, seed and runtime digest; an asserter posts the verdict with a bond; and anyone can re-execute and challenge. That turns "AI judgment" into deterministic compute with a fraud proof. It proves the model said something. It does not prove the statement is true.

### Data ingress: prices are solved, the web is a trusted key, TEEs are a physical-security bet

Price data is a cheap commodity. Pyth Core's update fee is **zero on all mainnet EVM chains** (its Hermes fetch API now needs a key) ([Pyth](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/current-fees)). Chainlink Data Streams settles Polymarket's 5- and 15-minute markets, which had passed **$7B in volume** by Q2 ([Chainlink Q2](https://chain.link/blog/quarterly-review-q2-2026)). Chainlink's CRE, which replaced Functions, runs developer-defined Go/TypeScript workflows with HTTP consensus across DON nodes ([CRE docs](https://docs.chain.link/cre.md)). The 2026 oracle failures hit custom, self-operated feeds rather than the major networks. At Ostium on Arbitrum (July 15), CoinDesk reports that an authorized forwarder submitted **future-dated timestamps** and drained about $18M ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/07/15/ostium-suffers-usd18-million-exploit-as-oracle-attack-wave-continues-to-hit-defi)). DefiLlama's hack list records the same event as a $23.8M private-key compromise ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)), and the two accounts were not reconciled. Either way, a resolver must pin signer sets at market creation and reject timestamps outside `[deadline − ε, deadline]`.

"zkTLS" is a misnomer for on-chain purposes. TLSNotary's own team writes that a web proof is "checked by whoever trusts the verifier that witnessed it". The witness must be present live, so a proof about past web content cannot be made after the fact, and contracts end up verifying **a notary signature** ([TLSNotary](https://tlsnotary.org/blog/2026/06/17/public-verifiability)). Proxy mode takes about 1–2 s and MPC mode about 4–15 s ([TLSNotary benchmarks](https://tlsnotary.org/blog/2026/05/10/blog-proxy-mode)). Reclaim's new protocol attests a 1,200 KB response in 7.28 s on a phone ([ePrint 2026/2187](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2187)). Peer (formerly ZKP2P), the flagship production app, **abandoned zkTLS for AWS Nitro enclaves**, cutting latency from ~30 s to under 1 s ([TLSNotary](https://tlsnotary.org/blog/2026/06/23/where-trust-lives); [Peer docs](https://docs.peer.xyz/protocol/v3/v3-overview)). In a market the prover is also a counterparty, so a self-chosen notary is worthless. Web inputs must therefore be M-of-N attestations from keys pinned at creation, wrapped as bonded claims.

TEEs are the cheapest way to run hidden code, but 2025 research (WireTap, Battering RAM, TEE.fail) showed that **someone with physical access to the server and inexpensive equipment can extract Intel and AMD attestation keys and forge quotes**. Because NVIDIA GPU attestation is anchored in the CPU's confidential VM, GPU attestation inherits the same weakness. Intel and AMD consider the attack out of scope ([tee.fail](https://tee.fail/)). BuilderNet's docs now say its nodes are *not* running in TEEs while it moves to a new security model ([BuilderNet](https://buildernet.org/docs/)). On-chain verification is affordable: about 350k gas for a ZK-wrapped DCAP quote ([Automata](https://blog.ata.network/automatas-release-of-dcap-attestation-v1-1-for-agentic-systems-84ae98900370)) and about 260k for AWS Nitro via SP1 ([Espresso](https://docs.espressosys.com/network/concepts/rollup-developers/integrating-an-optimistic-rollup/zk-attestation-verification)). In a permissionless market the operator can be a counterparty. TEEs belong behind value-at-risk caps, multi-vendor quorums and challenge windows, never as the root of trust.

Randomness has a clear default. drand quicknet verifies on L1 for **131–141k gas** since Pectra, needs no request or callback, and its round number is a deterministic function of time ([drand](https://docs.drand.love/blog/2025/08/26/verifying-bls12-on-ethereum)), so it doubles as a **chain-independent clock**. Chainlink VRF v2.5 charges a 20–84% premium on callback gas ([Chainlink](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/supported-networks)). With Pyth Entropy the provider can see a result and withhold it, which is fatal if the provider is a participant ([Pyth](https://docs.pyth.network/entropy/protocol-design)). PREVRANDAO gives each proposer about one bit of influence per slot ([EIP-4399](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4399)).

### Sealed information: every timelock is a committee

Nothing on mainnet unlocks "at block N" without trusting someone. drand **tlock** lets anyone encrypt offline to a future round, using Boneh–Franklin IBE with round numbers as identities. Its quicknet runs 18 organizations on 22 nodes, and a colluding threshold could decrypt early ([drand docs](https://docs.drand.love/docs/timelock-encryption)). For agent markets tlock does one important thing: it **removes the free option in commit-reveal**, because a committer who sees they are losing cannot simply withhold the reveal. Shutter's API on Gnosis supports time- and event-triggered key release but warns it is "not for high-value assets" ([Shutter](https://docs.shutter.network/docs/protocol/api)). Zama is the only FHE system with real money on Ethereum. It runs at **~20 TPS**, uses a 13-node threshold KMS whose MPC runs inside Nitro enclaves, and holds more than $90M shielded ([Zama litepaper](https://docs.zama.org/protocol/zama-protocol-litepaper); [Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/zama-newsletter-september-recap-confidential-defi-at-scale-the-defillama-report-zama-on-solana-and-more)). FHE hides data, not code. A May 2026 court order froze the USDC behind Zama's whole cUSDC wrapper because of a single deposit ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/the-freeze-on-zamas-cusdc-contract-has-been-lifted-and-all-systems-are-back-to-normal)), a shared-fate warning against pooling all escrow in one freezable wrapper. Arcium's dishonest-majority MPC went live on Solana on Feb 2, 2026. Privacy holds if one node is honest, but **any faulty node aborts the computation**, so a market that must resolve by a deadline needs a timeout path ([Arcium docs](https://docs.arcium.com/multi-party-execution-environments-mxes/mpc-protocols.md)). On Aug 12, 2026 Nillion pivoted to "Covenants", which it describes as "an encrypted instruction that decrypts itself when a condition is met". That is almost exactly a sealed resolver, but slashing is not due until 2027 and the Sept 28 mainnet launch is unconfirmed ([Nillion](https://nillion.com/news/the-road-to-encrypted-markets/)). Fhenix CoFHE was reported to launch on Ethereum and Arbitrum today, Oct 21 ([Incrypted](https://incrypted.com/en/fhenix-will-launch-its-mainnet-with-support-for-confidential-tokens-on-october-21/)), while its homepage still said "coming soon" when fetched ([Fhenix](https://www.fhenix.io/)). Treat it as unconfirmed.

### Agent rails: identity, payment, permission and intents exist but carry no trust

The rails exist, but none of them carries a trust signal. ERC-8004's Identity Registry is a cheap namespace. Feedback in its Reputation Registry almost never carries proof of payment (0–1.3% of records do), and its Validation Registry is effectively unused. One audit found **0 requests on Ethereum and 13 on Base**, and another source found no deployment at all, which is an unresolved conflict ([LLM4Agents](https://llm4agents.com/blog/erc-8004-registries-onchain-audit); [arXiv 2606.26028](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26028v2)). x402 V2 adds header-based payments, extensions, a Discovery API and four schemes (`exact`, `upto`, `batch-settlement`, `auth-capture`), plus an `escrow` flow. It has **no conditional or refund semantics** ([x402 spec](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md)), and the Linux Foundation's x402 Foundation became operational in July with 40 members ([Pondero](https://pondero.ai/agents/guides/x402-vs-acp-vs-ap2-agent-payment-protocols-october-2026/)). Tempo, live since March 18, offers MPP sessions over payment channels plus protocol-level **access keys with spend limits, expiry and call scopes** ([Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/developers)). Circle Arc launched Sept 16 with USDC as gas ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260916108863/en/Circle-Launches-Arc-Mainnet-an-Economic-Operating-System-for-the-Internet)). MetaMask Advanced Permissions ship ERC-7715 requests and ERC-7710 redemption, with on-chain caveat enforcers and **sub-delegation**, across 12 chains ([MetaMask](https://metamask.io/news/introducing-advanced-permissions)). One custom caveat enforcer gives a human a revocable, on-chain-enforced agent budget: "only `take()` on contracts whose resolver hash is in set S, at most $N per day." Coinbase's older AgentKit, by contrast, still **enforces no spend caps** by default ([GitHub](https://github.com/coinbase/agentkit)).

### Disputes, bonds and slashing: every backstop has a ceiling

Optimistic oracles dominate, and their ceiling is capture at the final vote. In March 2025 a UMA holder with **about 25% of the vote** resolved a $7M Polymarket market YES although no deal had occurred ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented)). In most 2026 votes the **top 10 wallets cast more than half** of all votes ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-dispute-resolution-system-under-scrutiny-as-uma-voting-raises-fraud-concerns)). UMA's response was to whitelist *proposers* (37 at launch; one unofficial guide says 177 later), which left the *voters* untouched ([UMA](https://blog.uma.xyz/articles/managed-proposers)). Reality.eth's bond-doubling escalation is fully permissionless and explicitly warns about "tricksy" questions ([Reality.eth](https://realitio.github.io/docs/html/whitepaper.html)). Kleros v2 runs natively on Arbitrum ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/introducing-kleros-2-0-beta-decentralized-justice-built-on-arbitrum-one/28136)). GenLayer's multi-LLM validator court costs about $0.50 a ruling but is still on testnet, targeting mainnet in Q4 2026 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/genlayer-ai-court-validators/)). Augur's Lituus reboot ran its first end-to-end fork in July but has no general-use date ([augur.net](https://www.augur.net/)). The cheapest credible backstop for natural-language disputes is statistical. Independent multi-model voting that auto-resolves only unanimous, high-confidence questions reached **97.87% accuracy on 47% of questions** and routed the rest elsewhere ([arXiv 2605.30802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30802)).

EigenLayer's slashing has been live since April 17, 2025. Two secondary sources say 2026; the official post says 2025 ([Eigen Labs](https://www.eigenlabs.org/blog/slashing-goes-live/)). It now supports **redistributable** operator sets, which send slashed funds to a fixed recipient ([EigenCloud docs](https://docs.eigencloud.xyz/eigenlayer/concepts/slashing/slashing-concept)). That is exactly what is needed to compensate takers harmed by a malicious resolver. The caveats are heavy: whoever holds both the slashing key and the recipient can drain the set, slashed shares clear only after a 7-day delay, and no major slash had been confirmed by mid-2026 ([Protofire](https://protofire.io/guides/restaking-protocols/)).

## Ethereum, Base and Arbitrum are each shipping a piece of the kernel

### Ethereum L1: proof-carrying resolution now, censorship-resistant settlement in 2027

Mainnet already supports **oracle-free resolution of any fact from roughly the last 27 hours**. EIP-2935 keeps 8191 block hashes in state, and EIP-4788 keeps 8191 beacon roots. Against those anchors a resolver can verify Merkle proofs of past storage, transactions, receipts or beacon facts, including a validator being slashed or the proposer schedule (EIP-7917) ([EIP-2935](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2935); [EIP-4788](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4788)). A permissionless "checkpoint" function that stores a hash before the window closes extends this to long-dated markets. Fusaka (Dec 3, 2025) added a **P-256 precompile at `0x100` costing 6,900 gas** (passkeys, secure enclaves, some attestation chains) and a hard **per-transaction cap of 16,777,216 gas**. The cap stays even as blocks grow, so heavy resolvers must verify proofs rather than execute on L1 ([EF](https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/11/06/fusaka-mainnet-announcement); [EF gas cap](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/10/21/fusaka-gascap-update)).

Glamsterdam went live on Sepolia on Oct 6 and reaches Hoodi on Oct 26. It has **no mainnet date**; EF Protocol's planning assumes December 2026 ([EF Hoodi](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/16/glamsterdam-hoodi-announcement); [EF priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities)). It brings ePBS, block-level access lists, a 2-gas **`SLOTNUM`** opcode that survives future slot-length changes ([EIP-7843](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7843)), 64 KiB contracts, a canonical CREATE2 factory so a resolver gets **the same address on every EVM chain** (EIP-7997), and state repricing that makes fresh per-position storage slots and `transfer`/`send` stipend payouts breakage risks ([EF repricing](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/08/24/glamsterdam-repricing-testing)). Core devs aligned on a credible **200M gas floor** after Glamsterdam ([EF](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/02/soldogn-interop-recap)).

Hegotá (2027) is where the protocol gets real guarantees. **FOCIL** forces eligible transactions into blocks within about a slot ([EIP-7805](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7805)). That matters most for time-sensitive settlement, because until it ships, settlement windows must absorb builder censorship. **Frame Transactions** (EIP-8141) bring native account abstraction with paymasters, atomic batches and pluggable (including post-quantum) signatures ([EIP-8141](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141)). A-tier companions include keyed nonces for parallel position lanes, a `PAY` opcode for transfers without reentrancy, and **transaction assertions** (EIP-7906). The EF explicitly pitches assertions for agent delegation, and a protocol can require that a call "comes from a frame transaction containing the exact required assertion" ([EF assertions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions); [EF tier list](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-hegota-eips)). A market could therefore demand that every take carry a canonical assertion such as "taker's max loss ≤ X and no approvals changed." Drafts worth tracking include EIP-8209 commit-reveal frames (a COMMIT in block N−1 guarantees inclusion of the REVEAL in block N) ([EIP-8209](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8209)) and the LUCID/EIP-8105 encrypted mempools, which are unscheduled ([EIP-8184](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8184)). The standards layer also supplies resolver input formats: ERC-7992 (ZKML), ERC-7913 (verifying signatures from address-less keys, Final), ERC-7969 (DKIM), ERC-8002 (Bitcoin SPV) and ERC-8033 (bonded commit-reveal agent councils for subjective questions) ([ERC-8033](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8033)). The EF's **zkAPI** went live this month: private, dollar-capped API credits that agents spend via Groth16 proofs from an on-chain vault ([EF](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/01/introducing-zkapi)). Further out, the EF Strawmap targets post-quantum L1 security by **December 2029** ([strawmap.org](https://strawmap.org)). Long-dated markets should verify signatures through ERC-1271/7913 rather than raw `ecrecover`.

### Base: conditional transactions at the sequencer

Base left the OP Stack in February 2026 for its own codebase ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbases-base-shifts-away-from-op-stack-in-major-layer-2-strategy-pivot/)), so Superchain interop should not be assumed. Its Cobalt upgrade (Sept 30) shipped the single most relevant new L2 primitive: **Validity Transactions**. A signed transaction submitted with criteria (a pool price, a block-number bound, a flashblock index) "remain[s] dormant until user-specified criteria are met" and stays private until it lands ([Base](https://base.org/blog/cobalt)). Agents can pre-sign takes conditioned on chain state, and settlers can pre-sign "resolve at block ≥ deadline" with no keeper. These transactions are sequencer-trusted and specific to Base, and the docs on fees and on what criteria can express were not retrieved. B20 tokenized stocks pair each token with a **Chainlink total-return feed** and handle corporate actions through a multiplier rather than rebasing, so resolvers must read the multiplier, not raw balances ([Base](https://base.org/blog/b20-tokenized-stocks-on-base)).

### Arbitrum: disputes, derivatives and a paused WASM engine

The Security Council paused new Stylus activations after "sophisticated attacks on hand-crafted WASM programs". The stated path to reopening restricts hand-crafted WASM ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/security-council-emergency-action-2-10-2026/31530)), which directly conflicts with "anyone uploads a WASM resolver." A single audited interpreter contract, or the zkVM route, avoids this. Priority Gas Auctions plus Fast Feed went live Oct 7 ([Arbitrum Foundation](https://blog.arbitrum.foundation/)). The prior Timeboost study found **3 entities won 99.74% of express-lane auctions** ([arXiv 2509.22143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22143)), a warning against first-come takes. Arbitrum's real assets are derivatives and courts. **Variational** runs about $1B of open interest in P2P derivatives, and its Pro tier lets institutions "define their own derivatives… and escrow collateral in segregated on-chain contracts" ([Variational](https://docs.variational.io/)). That makes it both the nearest institutional analogue and a hedge venue. Pendle's **Boros** trades funding rates on crypto, gold, silver and the S&P 500 ([Pendle](https://pendlefi.substack.com/p/pendle-print-107)), giving resolvers usable variables. **Robinhood Chain** has been live since July 1 and already out-volumes Base on DEXs (**$34.2B vs $30.0B over 30 days**), mostly on Uniswap v4 ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/overview/dexs/Robinhood%20Chain); [Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/arbitrumdao-factsheet-robinhood-chain-mainnet-launch/31041)).

### Incumbents doing new things: escrow, auctions and credit that fit deadlines

Uniswap is now the most interesting incumbent for this design. Beyond The Compact, its **Continuous Clearing Auctions** clear bids at a single uniform price per block and call an optional **`validate()` hook on every bid** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Uniswap/continuous-clearing-auction)). Uniswap Labs also runs governance-upgradeable first-party hooks. StablePair prices peg-restoring flow with a **Dutch-auction fee that decays each block** and did $3.1B in four weeks; VolatilePair sets asymmetric buy and sell fees ([Uniswap](https://blog.uniswap.org/stablepair-hook-a-fee-that-moves-with-the-market); [Uniswap](https://blog.uniswap.org/volatilepair-hook-pricing-both-sides-of-the-trade)). v4 is now Ethereum's top DEX by 30-day volume ([DefiLlama](https://api.llama.fi/overview/dexs/Ethereum)). Morpho **Midnight** brings signed-offer, fixed-term credit whose markets have immutable maturities, and Coinbase distributes it ([Morpho docs](https://docs.morpho.org/build/midnight/get-started/)). A Midnight market whose maturity equals a contract's deadline is a natural way to finance positions. 1inch **Aqua** does $3.9B a month with liquidity that never leaves LP wallets, verified by post-callback balance checks ([GitHub](https://github.com/1inch/aqua)). The same pattern would let a posted stake earn yield until someone takes it. Some incumbents are poor building blocks for now: Aave V4 spokes are DAO-gated ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/395617/aave-v4-launches-ethereum-mainnet)), Euler is retrenching toward zero fees ([Euler](https://www.euler.finance/blog/euler-q1-2026)), and Unichain, with about $30M TVL, is handing operations to Optimism ([Uniswap](https://blog.uniswap.org/unichain-is-upgrading-to-op-enterprise)).

## Played forward: existence claims are the unoccupied frontier

### The contract types this stack makes possible

The deepest structural fact is that ZK cheaply proves **existential** statements. Universal claims ("no input breaks P") can still be traded by making them asymmetric. The poster stakes that no witness will appear before the deadline and wins by default if none does; the taker wins atomically by submitting a witness the resolver checks. The price of the "exists" side then becomes a continuous, agent-traded probability of failure. That reframing, together with the rails above, yields contract types that no venue runs today.

| Contract type | Resolver | Composes with | Closest prior art | White space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-sided security market ("no input violates invariant I of contract C before T") | Replay a witness on forked state, or a sanitizer-instrumented binary in a zkVM; the journal carries crash kind and site, never the input | Immunefi-paying protocols as the "no" side; reinsurers; Morpho curators reading the price | Human-triaged bounties; zkpoex (research) | Unoccupied: no production auto-verifying payout found |
| Hidden-test procurement | Buyer commits H(tests), tlock-reveals at the deadline; zk or optimistic proof that the code passes | Buyer-seeded subsidy; Boundless callback | Bittensor Ridges, Arkhai Git Commit Marketplace | Partially: confidentiality is trusted today |
| Resolver meta-bounty + underwriting | "A state exists where this resolver diverges from its declared spec" | Redistributable EigenLayer sets paying the market escrow | None found | Unoccupied |

The security market is the clearest wedge, and it is defensive at heart. Human-triaged bounties are breaking under AI-generated volume. curl ended its bounty in January 2026 after its confirmed-vulnerability rate fell **below 5%** ([Daniel Stenberg](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/01/26/the-end-of-the-curl-bug-bounty/)), and Code4rena wound down in May ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/401179/immufefi-absorb-code4rena-bug-bounty-customers-shutdown-decision)). A resolver that executes a submission and checks a predicate needs no triage, and invalid claims simply fail against a submission bond. The payoff gap is institutional: the A1 authors estimate attackers profit at exploit values around $6,000 where defenders need $60,000, because bounties are capped near 10% while attackers capture everything ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/10/ai_agents_automatically_steal_cryptocurrency/)). A pre-committed, auto-paying stake narrows that gap. How capable autonomous agents really are is contested. Anthropic's SCONE-bench found models reproduce about half of historical exploits in simulation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/smart-contracts)), while a contamination-free 2026 re-evaluation found **no end-to-end exploitation in 110 agent-incident pairs** ([arXiv 2603.10795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10795)). That disagreement is itself a high-information, code-resolvable question to make markets on. The design must keep the witness private until payment. That means local proving, since delegated proving leaks the witness. The ideal is verifiable encryption of the witness to the payer, which zkpoex lists as still "under research" ([zkpoex](https://ziemann.me/zkpoex/)). AIxCC-style systems produce exactly the proof-of-vulnerability blobs takers would hold ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results)).

Several compositions are less obvious than any single row. **Resolver-hash event identity plus CTF semantics** lets two venues listing the same resolver, inputs and deadline pool liquidity automatically, and lets Morpho Blue list position tokens by ID. That fixes the law-of-one-price gap by construction. **Every market born with a meta-bounty** means a slice of the creator's stake pays any agent who proves the resolver reads creator-mutable storage, an upgradeable proxy, or a single-block price. Takers can then filter for markets whose meta-bounty has stood unclaimed for N blocks, so AI auditors clear out the lemons before capital arrives. **Underwriting premiums become a public trust score**: when third-party operators put redistributable restaked capital behind a resolver, the premium they charge prices that resolver's honesty. **Settlement-MEV recapture** follows Chainlink SVR and API3 OEV ([Chainlink Q2](https://chain.link/blog/quarterly-review-q2-2026)): auction the right to submit the resolving report and route the proceeds to the losing side, instead of letting agents race. **drand as clock** expresses deadlines as beacon rounds, so one market can settle consistently across chains whose sequencer timestamps disagree. **Solvers as underwriters** comes from wrapping each take as an ERC-7683 resolved order whose `assumptions` name the resolver hash and whose `Witness` variable carries the proof. Solvers with perp or Boros hedges then become default liquidity, and ERC-7710 sub-delegation lets a fund agent hand capped budgets to specialist sub-agents. **Stake that earns until taken** uses Aqua-style pull, The Compact for custody, and a Midnight loan maturing at the deadline, which together form an agent-native carry stack across Base and Ethereum. Finally, Vitalik's **distilled human judgment**, in which a random human jury scores k of N items and AIs answer everything ([Vitalik](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/28/aihumans.html)), becomes a resolver type for subjective questions with a small human budget.

Playing the agents forward means expecting adversarial invention first. Spore.fun's reproducing agents produced a real co-evolutionary arms race within one quarter: snipers bought up launch supply, and the agents evolved randomized-abort launches ([arXiv 2506.04236](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04236v2)). Project Vend's shopkeeper agents reached spontaneously for a forward contract on onions, which is illegal in the US ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Agents *will* use conditional contracts unprompted, often in ways nobody anticipated. The grounded version of "let agents discover use cases" is to make adversarial discovery **profitable inside the rules**: meta-bounties, security markets and challenge fees pay for exactly the behavior that otherwise attacks the protocol.

### Novelty check: the hook exists, the market around it does not

A follow-up check on Oct 21 used three parallel researchers and primary sources: contract code, RPC reads, DefiLlama's 135 prediction-market listings and about 125 GitHub queries. Notes are in `research_notes/Novelty check arbitrary resolvers/`. It found **no live, used product with all four properties**: permissionless creation, a resolver supplied by the creator, two-sided escrow, and code-decided resolution that anyone can trigger.

- **Contract-level match, unused.** Sapience's `PredictionMarketEscrow` lets each pick name any `IConditionResolver` with no allowlist, and `settle()` is permissionless. Its code comments anticipate malicious resolvers. All 3,292 picks on its Ethereal deployment use Sapience's own resolvers: a Polymarket mirror, Pyth Lazer, UMA via LayerZero, and a manual resolver. That deployment has been dormant since Aug 5. The successor "Meridian" on Robinhood Chain is live, but its source was removed from the public repo on Aug 3, so whether it keeps open resolvers is unverified.
- **Dormant full matches (2020–23).** Opium v1/v2 is the closest. Each position has its own payout and oracle contracts, both sides post margin, and there is an `endTime`. Ante has stakers and challengers on an arbitrary test contract, but no deadline. Cozy offers protection markets on arbitrary trigger contracts. None was agent-native or pinned the resolver as a program.
- **Pluggable, but gated or trusted in practice.** Hedgehog (creator-chosen resolver in 2022; every live market used one platform wallet), Myriad (per-market oracle contract, admin-chosen), Polymarket V2's OracleAggregator (operator-only), Seer (Reality.eth hardwired), Gensyn Delphi (model plus prompt, single owner-set relayer, pooled stakes), and Hyperliquid HIP-4 (deployer actions are testnet-only and need a 500K HYPE stake).
- **One-sided code-verified escrows (2026).**
  - Agent Bounties on Base: 73 bounties and about $73 paid (self-reported), with every settlement going through quorum.
  - Alkahest: 43 mainnet contracts and zero transactions.
  - Reckn: SP1-gated escrow, testnet only.
  - Standards and research: ERC-8183, the ERC-8414 proposal, HoneyPause and zkpoex.
- **No match.** Trueo (formerly Truemarkets), Opinion, predict.fun, Rain (anyone can create markets, but resolution is by creator, AI or human oracle), Azuro, Overtime, Limitless, Manifold, and Drift BET (defunct since April).

The pattern matters more than any single venue. Pluggable resolvers keep recurring, and each time they end up gated, trusted in practice, or unused. That supports the thesis that the scarce part is the anti-lemons and discovery layer, not the hook. Residual doubt is about 20%, covering Colosseum (Solana), parts of the ETHGlobal showcase, closed-source Base apps, Delphi's current creation policy, and Meridian's source. Designs worth borrowing:

- Sapience's `IConditionResolver` interface, for compatibility.
- Reckn's `verifierCodeHash` pinning, re-checked at settlement.
- Ante's challenger-triggered resolution.
- Opium's split between payout logic and oracle per position.

Still not researched: Jito restaking, the Lagrange/Gevulot/Fermah prover networks, Privy/Turnkey/Safe/ERC-7579 policy engines, LayerZero/CCIP/Hyperlane, and fully trustless timelocks (VDFs, witness encryption).

## Eight ways code-resolved markets fail, and the architecture that contains them

**Resolver bugs are the dominant risk, ahead of oracle failure.** The Trail of Bits forgery, zkVM soundness bugs at roughly 100-bit security, and Aztec's Alpha V5 all point the same way. Aztec disclosed a critical proving-system bug, found by internal **AI-assisted auditing**, whose exploitation "cannot be ruled out", and it told users to treat V5 funds as exposed ([Aztec](https://aztec.network/blog/alpha-v5-proving-system-vulnerability)). Private state also prevented anyone from detecting exploitation after the fact. The containment layers are: mandatory reproducible builds tied to the image ID; strict witness validation (no unchecked deserialization); k-of-n zkVM verification for large markets (two independent zkVMs roughly double gas to ~540k but remove single-prover soundness risk); per-market value caps scaled to each trust root; and the meta-bounty, which pays agents to find these bugs before stakes lock.

**Lemons come from the gap between description and code.** LLM takers read natural-language descriptions. A malicious poster will write a description that diverges from the bytecode, the "Freysa inverted" honeypot. Free-text fields indexed for discovery also become injection channels into every agent that browses them; memory injection beats prompt-injection defenses in CrAIBench ([arXiv 2503.16248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16248)). The fix is to make the resolver the only canonical artifact. Ship machine-readable specs and symbolic summaries, have taker agents simulate the resolver locally, and route only true "spec disputes" to a subjective layer. The ordering of that layer: multi-model unanimity first, then ERC-8033-style bonded councils, Kleros or GenLayer, and finally INVALID. Never a token vote, given UMA's capture record.

**Input manipulation concentrates at the deadline.** A Stanford/SMU study found **821 accounts earned about $8.2M** on Polymarket 5-minute markets by moving the reference spot price in the final seconds. Polymarket switched to a 30-second TWAP ([Bitcoin Foundation](https://bitcoinfoundation.org/news/prediction-markets/polymarket-btc-price-manipulation/); [polymarkets.co.il](https://polymarkets.co.il/en/news/polymarket-bitcoin-twap-settlement-fix)). A thin-market feed pump drained Nostra on Starknet ([shattered.io](https://shattered.io/nostra-finance-3-5-million-pragma-oracle-exploit-2026/)). Architectural fixes: inputs are TWAPs or medians over windows ending at the deadline; signed reports must have timestamps inside the window; and a **causality filter** forbids data a single participant can cheaply move or cause, such as thin pools, single-signer feeds, posts and commits, except in self-commitment markets. Reflexive markets on the venue's own metrics will be as thin as MetaDAO's (median about 12 traders per decision), so they need long windows or should be disallowed.

**Settlement atomicity and timing are solved by construction, if escrow comes first.** The "Ghost Fill" literature documents **1.95M reverted Polymarket settlements**, half of them deliberate. The cause was off-chain matching followed by later on-chain settlement, which let order owners invalidate preconditions such as allowances, balances and receiver hooks in between. The paper puts realized profit at no less than $1.49M; its conclusion says $2.95M, an internal inconsistency ([arXiv 2606.16852](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16852v1)). The companion study found 97.2% of reverts traced to preconditions the owner controls, and all three other audited markets were vulnerable ([Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2609.17902)). A fully on-chain protocol that locks both stakes at post and take, with **pull-based claims instead of pushed ERC-1155 transfers** (the receiver hook was a vector), removes the whole class. Remaining timing risks are a pre-deadline lockout sized to each chain's gap between pre-confirmation and finality (Base pre-confirmations lead block sealing by up to 1.8 s ([Base docs](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/flashblocks/apps))), the ~27-hour proof window, and builder censorship until FOCIL.

**Latency races favor the fastest acceptable taker, not the best price.** Microsoft's Magentic Marketplace found that every model shows severe first-proposal bias, making **response speed worth 10–30× quality** ([arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25779)). Combined with Arbitrum's three-firm express-lane concentration, first-come takes hand the market to infrastructure rather than to models. The fix is in-contract frequent batch auctions: sealed takes per epoch, forced reveal via tlock, uniform-odds clearing (CoW/Angstrom logic applied to odds), or a sealed Vickrey auction for the right to take. Project Deal adds a quieter effect. Stronger models extracted measurably better prices while counterparties rated fairness identically, so **value transfers silently** from weaker agents ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/project-deal)). LLM pricing agents also drift toward supracompetitive outcomes without instruction ([arXiv 2404.00806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00806v5)), which argues for open entry and short-lived contracts.

**Sybil reputation is worthless unless stake-weighted and costly to reset.** Optimism's futarchy filtered **4,122 suspected bots against 430 retained forecasters** ([Optimism](https://gov.optimism.io/t/futarchy-v1-preliminary-findings/10062)), and ERC-8004 scores move for fractions of a cent. Liquefaction shows that TEE-encumbered keys let addresses be secretly rented or pooled, so "one address, one actor" is unenforceable ([arXiv 2412.02634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02634)). Gatta et al. formalize the escape route: reputation disciplines behavior only when resetting identity is costly, and slashable stake complements it ([arXiv 2609.02992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02992)). The design follows: **bond-as-identity** with an unbonding delay, reputation weighted by stake and counterparty diversity, fees that make self-dealing negative-EV, and no volume incentives ever. As a byproduct, every settlement can be exported as payment-proven ERC-8004 feedback, and the resolver can act as a `validationResponse` submitter. That fills the empty Validation Registry and makes the protocol the grounded reputation source the agent ecosystem lacks. Paid verifier roles invite free-riding (Bittensor's weight copying ([Bittensor docs](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor))), which is another argument for deterministic resolvers.

**Harm markets are a data-class problem, not a moderation problem.** War- and death-linked contracts drew **$529M** on Polymarket in 2026 amid insider-trading scrutiny ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/01/nx-s1-5731568/polymarket-trade-iran-supreme-leader-killing)), and Kalshi bans markets directly tied to death or war ([Northeastern](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/elections-death-war-where-should-regulators-draw-the-line-with-prediction-markets/)). A permissionless protocol cannot moderate markets, but it can restrict which **data classes** its resolver VM accepts. The VM can expose only typed adapters: signed price reports, drand, chain-state proofs, and attestations of specific endpoint classes. Events caused by actors then become hard to express for anyone other than the actor. This is honest containment, not prevention. Any web-attestation adapter can carry a news API's answer to a harmful question, so the protocol also needs a policy on which endpoint classes its canonical adapters accept, and indexers and front-ends will apply their own. The notes did not research this area deeply, so the legal exposure of a neutral protocol remains open.

**Dependencies and compliance can freeze settlement.** Issuer controls (B20 `seizeWithMemo`, pause switches, Uniswap Permissioned Pools' `unwindPosition`), stablecoin freezes like Zama's cUSDC case, and operator-run sequencers can all halt a resolution path. Segregated escrow per market, collateral choices declared per market, and INVALID-and-refund timeouts keep a single legal action or dead dependency from becoming protocol-wide loss.

## Conclusion

The shift in understanding is that the scarce asset is no longer compute, data or payments; all of them are commodities with known prices. What is scarce is a **credible commitment that a program, and only that program, decides**, together with machinery that makes bad programs expensive to post. In this protocol the anti-lemons layer *is* the product: canonical resolver identity, meta-bounties, underwriting premiums, and refusing to fall back on votes. The best first market is not the one that sounds most like a prediction market. It is the one where existing institutions visibly fail and a resolver is unambiguous: existence claims about code (security and hidden-test procurement), paid for by sponsors who already spend real money on audits and evaluations. Those markets also produce the protocol's most valuable byproduct, a stake-weighted, payment-proven performance record for agents and resolvers that ERC-8004 currently cannot generate.

The practical sequence follows from what is live versus scheduled. The novelty check found that the hook exists (Sapience, Opium, Ante) but nobody runs the market around it. The next step is therefore to build a self-contained kernel on Base or Solana, studying those contracts first: escrow-first, pull-claims, RISC-V resolvers by image ID, typed input adapters, batch-sealed takes. Expose it through ERC-8183 events, ERC-7683 orders, x402 discovery and an MCP server, so agents find it without new integration. Treat Hegotá-era features (FOCIL, Frames, assertions) as 2027 upgrades rather than dependencies. The thesis that "agents invent the use case" has precedent in 2026 so far only in the adversarial direction: snipers, memory poisoners, wash traders. A protocol where those inventions pay inside the rules is the most realistic way to let the useful surprises arrive.
